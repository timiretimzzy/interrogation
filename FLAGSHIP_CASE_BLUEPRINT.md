# Flagship Case Blueprint — The Last Broadcast

## Scope

This is a narrative and investigative blueprint for Phase 4.3.0, not case data. IDs,
dialogue, UI changes, engine changes, and JSON are deliberately deferred to Phase 4.3.1.

## Stage A — Case concept exploration and selection

| Concept | Premise and surface mystery | Hidden complexity and investigative tension | Layered discovery, theories, and reinterpretation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The Last Broadcast** (selected) | At 9:12 p.m., celebrated investigative radio host Mara Venn dies alone in Studio B during a live charity broadcast. Her producer Elias Ward was heard arguing with her and has an apparent opportunity. | The broadcast was not live. Mara died from a delayed poison in a throat lozenge she selected herself; the “argument” was a recorded rehearsal used by Mara to create an alibi for a source. Her death resulted from a blackmail negotiation that a trusted colleague redirected into murder. | Audio, studio access, health, finance, and personal-relationship branches each support credible but incomplete theories. The central aha is that the recording which seems to establish Elias's presence instead proves Mara anticipated being absent at 9:12—and was already dead. |
| **The Empty Archive** | A city archivist vanishes after opening a sealed municipal collection; a security video shows her leaving alone with a box. | She uncovered a decades-old land fraud, staged a departure to protect a whistleblower, then was involuntarily confined by a panicked descendant attempting to recover a document. | Strong document and custody puzzle, but fewer face-to-face pressure opportunities: much of the decisive reasoning risks becoming archival reconstruction rather than interrogation. |
| **Winter Garden** | A philanthropist collapses at a conservatory gala after publicly changing her will; three heirs had access to the champagne. | The collapse is an allergic reaction deliberately induced through a floral perfume, while the will announcement was itself bait to expose a trustee's embezzlement. | Rich social motives and environmental clues, but the method is comparatively obscure and risks a medical-detail accusation rather than a broadly legible reinterpretation. |

**Selection:** *The Last Broadcast* is the strongest flagship candidate. Its central object,
a recording, naturally supports selection-based questioning, independently meaningful
branches, response revision under evidence, and a fair re-reading of an early “fact.”
It supports multiple suspects without making every character culpable and makes the
engine's explicit central deduction meaningful without solving the accusation.

**Stage A report:** Three materially different cases were considered; *The Last Broadcast*
best combines distributed knowledge, contradictions, multiple routes, replayable order,
and an earned cross-branch realization.

## Stage B — Target player experience

1. **Orientation:** The player arrives after a supposed live broadcast. Mara appears to
have collapsed in a locked studio; Elias, her volatile producer and former partner,
argued with her moments beforehand. The natural theory is an impulsive poisoning or
assault by Elias.
2. **Expansion:** Mara’s off-book investigation into charity donor Adrian Kells, a large
unexplained payment to sound engineer Priya Nair, and assistant June Hale’s concealed
late-night visit make blackmail, bribery, and a suicide-like overdose plausible.
3. **Friction:** Studio access shows Elias could have entered, but the lozenge tin has
only Mara’s prints. The broadcast clock, producer log, and audio file disagree about
what happened at 9:12. Priya’s payment was not hush money; June’s visit was not romantic.
4. **Reinterpretation:** The player learns the argument was a rehearsal recording and
recognizes it had aired as “live.” An apparent timestamped alibi becomes proof that Mara
planned not to speak live at the moment she died.
5. **Synthesis:** The player connects Mara’s planned absence, the lozenge selected from a
freshly refilled tin, and Adrian’s intercepted blackmail communications. The studio was
not a locked-room murder scene; it was a controlled place to make a delayed method look
like a medical collapse.
6. **Central aha:** Someone who knew both Mara’s private signal for a protected source and
her pre-recorded broadcast plan could replace one lozenge before the show. That person
was not Elias or Adrian but Priya, whom Mara trusted with both the edit and the signal.
7. **Explanation:** The accusation asks the player to account for killer, motive, method,
and the falsified “live” timeline; the satisfying answer explains the poison, payment,
audio, access, and every false lead together.

**Stage B report:** The intended arc moves from a familiar suspect to several credible
alternatives, then makes a known recording change meaning before asking the player to
build—not receive—the final explanation.

## Stage C — Canonical truth

Mara Venn had discovered that Adrian Kells diverted disaster-relief donations through a
shell consultancy. She intended to give Kells one final chance to return the money before
an exposé. She had separately promised an anonymous whistleblower that their identity
would be protected. Her private signal to that source was the phrase “keep the channel
open,” embedded in the closing of a pre-recorded rehearsal segment.

Priya Nair, Mara’s sound engineer and closest professional confidante, had secretly
received money from Kells months earlier to remove a damaging audio file from the station
archive. Priya initially believed it was a harmless reputation-management request. On
learning Mara had found the file and was about to expose the fraud, Priya feared both
prosecution and Mara’s discovery of her payment. Kells did not order a murder; he sent
Priya a threatening instruction to “make the file disappear before tonight,” intending
destruction of evidence. Priya chose murder alone.

At 8:35, Priya preloaded a rehearsal recording as the 9:00–9:18 broadcast and altered the
console’s status lamp so it would read LIVE. At 8:47, while ostensibly replacing the
studio’s standard lozenges at Mara’s request, she replaced one lemon lozenge with an
identical poisoned one containing aconitine extracted from an unlabelled ornamental
monkshood tincture in the station’s prop cabinet. Mara habitually chose the lemon lozenge
before her opening. Priya expected a rapid cardiac collapse after Mara entered Studio B.

Elias argued with Mara at 8:52 about postponing the Kells story; he left at 8:57 and
truthfully waited in the control-room corridor. June entered at 9:03 to return Mara’s
spare key and saw Mara take a lozenge, but did not see Priya’s earlier substitution.
Mara began the prerecorded program at 9:00, entered Studio B at 9:06 to await Kells’s
call, and collapsed at 9:11. The recording’s scheduled rehearsal argument played at
9:12. At 9:14, Priya found Mara, stopped the recording, and called for help. She deleted
the console edit history but overlooked the automated broadcast backup and a refilling
invoice showing the tin was newly supplied.

* Responsibility: Priya is the sole killer. Kells is guilty of fraud, coercive
intimidation, and attempted evidence suppression, not homicide.
* Motive: Priya killed to prevent exposure of her archive deletion and payment from Kells.
* Method and opportunity: a substituted lemon lozenge poisoned before the broadcast, made
possible by Priya’s trusted access to audio preparation, props, and Mara’s routine.
* Intentional concealment: pre-recorded program presented as live; edit-log deletion;
Priya’s past payment and archive deletion; June’s key return; Elias’s argument.
* Accident: Priya did not expect the automated off-console backup or the invoice to make
the replacement time and recording identifiable.
* Real evidence: toxicology, the lemon lozenge residue, backup audio waveform, playout
server schedule, invoice, archive deletion audit, Kells–Priya messages, June’s observation.
* Misleading evidence: Elias’s argument, Kells’s threat, the locked studio, Priya’s
payment, and the console LIVE lamp.
* Priya expects investigators to conclude either Elias poisoned Mara after the argument or
Kells arranged a direct murder. The largest mistaken assumption is that audio heard at
9:12 records an event at 9:12.

### Internal consistency audit

| Check | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Timeline | The poison is placed before Mara chooses it; collapse precedes the recorded argument; discovery and shutdown follow. |
| Motive | Priya’s personal exposure, rather than Kells’s threat alone, explains why she escalates to murder. |
| Physical possibility | A preloaded program can play independently; a delayed ingested poison permits Priya to be absent; automatic backup survives console-log deletion. |
| Knowledge boundaries | No witness sees both substitution and ingestion; only Priya knows the full plan; Kells knows coercion but not murder. |
| Evidence causality | Each decisive fact follows from a real action, while every misleading item describes a true but misread event. |

**Stage C report:** Canonical truth is complete and coherent. The false live timeline,
not an undisclosed late twist, is the case’s central mistaken assumption.

**Stage F report:** Each false theory explains real evidence and fails on a discoverable

## Stage K — Response and behavior model

Priya begins composed and technical, truthfully describing normal studio practice while
evasively minimizing the refill. She lies about live operation and discovery, then gives
narrow revised admissions when confronted by server/backup evidence. Her pressure points
are the archived deletion, payment, and the source cue; she never voluntarily supplies
the complete murder account.

Elias is defensive and emotionally candid in flashes: he initially conceals the key and
severity of argument, but truthfully identifies rehearsal after audio pressure. Kells is
controlled, admits neither fraud nor pressure until finance evidence makes denial
implausible, and distinguishes his demand to destroy a file from murder only after the
archive branch is established. June is anxious about her unrelated breach, truthful about
what she saw once key suspicion is resolved. Rowan gives medical facts but an initially
overcautious interpretation; Tomas deflects blame for retention failures.

Use `requires` for evidence-backed confrontations and conceptual pacing; use `excludes`
for superseded denials and closed theories. Weights can vary texture among equivalent
evasions but never govern critical disclosure or progression.

**Stage K report:** Eligibility models credible changing positions; all critical
information has deterministic reachable routes.

## Stage N — Fair misdirection

* **Elias:** His argument, key history, and lie genuinely explain relationship secrecy;
the corridor/recording limitation is discoverable and the lead closes as homicide while
retaining his useful rehearsal knowledge.
* **Kells:** Fraud, threat, payment, and planned call genuinely explain pressure on Mara
and Priya; message semantics plus insider method show he does not explain delivery or
audio control. His lead closes as a distinct non-homicide crime.
* **June:** Key access and evasiveness genuinely explain a policy breach; key log and
her ingestion observation make her a truth source instead of wasted suspicion.
* **Suicide/medical collapse:** Isolation, chosen lozenge, and Dr. Bell’s cautious
history make it reasonable; residue and replacement provenance make it impossible.

**Stage N report:** Every red herring illuminates a real part of the incident, has a
testable limitation, and closes with retained value.

## Stage P — Accusation blueprint

| Dimension | Canonical answer | Plausible wrong answers | Required proof and spoiler-safe diagnostic principle |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Who caused Mara’s death? | Priya Nair | Elias, Adrian Kells, Mara herself | Tampered-lozenge proof, Priya opportunity, and falsified-discovery evidence. A mismatch says the claim does not account for the delivery window and altered broadcast record, never names Priya. |
| Why? | To conceal her paid archive deletion and prevent Mara exposing her connection to Kells’s fraud | Jealousy, direct loyalty to Kells, martyrdom | Transfer, audit, and Kells message. A mismatch identifies an unexplained personal stake rather than revealing it. |
| How? | Substituted one lemon lozenge with aconitine before the broadcast | Poisoned drink, assault, deliberate overdose | Residue, ingestion timing, invoice/lot, and June observation. A mismatch notes failure to explain targeted ingestion and chain of custody. |
| How was the apparent timeline falsified? | A prerecorded rehearsal was aired as live while Priya manipulated playback records | Elias threatened Mara live; Kells called during broadcast; no falsification | Backup/rehearsal, schedule, and edit-history discrepancy. The accusation tests explanatory completeness: a correct name without proof fails, and
an answer that explains one branch but not the broadcast or delivery mechanism receives
only non-spoiling feedback.

**Stage P report:** Four meaningful dimensions cover responsibility, motive, method, and
the central explanatory mechanism; no dimension is trivia.

## Stage Q — Adversarial expectations

* **Tunnel vision:** An Elias-focused player can obtain the recording/corridor closure and
is redirected to timing and tampering; no necessary branch remains Elias-only.
* **Minimalist:** Obvious progress can reach proof, but not confidently explain Priya’s
personal motive without finance/archive work; accusation proof gates expose this gap
without spoiling it.
* **Completionist:** Extra inquiries clarify June, Kells, and Dr. Bell rather than
repeating facts; closed leads explicitly record their retained conclusion.
* **Contrarian:** Starting with Kells or Priya yields finance/archive or technical/audio
routes that converge at the same central deduction from distinct perspectives.
* **Random:** Critical revelations use reliable routes and OR-of-AND disclosure. Response
weighting may vary posture only; response exclusions retire contradictions rather than
stranding players.

**Stage Q report:** The blueprint predicts recovery paths and useful variation for biased
play styles; later simulation and human playtesting must validate those predictions.

## Stage R — Flagship acceptance blueprint

### Mechanical

The future information model and case must validate references, critical-fact reachability,
existential solution paths, universal progression safety, deduction prerequisites, theory
isolation, disclosure order, lead closure, proof-valid accusation, and spoiler-safe
diagnostics. It must undergo adversarial simulation review once available and must report
incomplete state-space exploration as unknown rather than a pass.

### Narrative

Acceptance requires canonical consistency; bounded character knowledge; three reasonable
false theories; evidence with reinterpretable value; productive contradictions; fair,
closed misdirection; cross-branch synthesis; and a central aha earned before the
explanatory accusation.

### Human experience

Playtests must establish that players understand what is available, feel agency, can
recover from incorrect theories, find false theories reasonable, recognize the aha as
earned, and experience the final answer as retrospectively inevitable.

**Stage R report:** Success criteria distinguish structural certification from narrative
fairness and human comprehension.
